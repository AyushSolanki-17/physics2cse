## Why this matters

Digital computers rely on controlled electrical differences. Before voltage, current, wires, switches, or memory make sense, we need one idea: matter has electrical properties that can be measured and rearranged.

Charge is the property that lets pieces of matter attract, repel, and influence each other electrically.

## What you should already know

You should know that a measured quantity needs a number and a unit from [Measurement, Models, and Units](/foundations/measurement-models-units/).

## The central intuition

Matter is made of particles. Some particles carry positive charge, some carry negative charge, and many objects contain both. When the positive and negative amounts balance, the object can look electrically neutral from far away.

Neutral does not mean "has no charge." It usually means "positive and negative charge cancel at the scale we are observing."

## The mechanism

Charges interact. Like signs repel, opposite signs attract. In ordinary materials, negatively charged electrons are often the charges that can move most easily. Positive atomic cores usually stay locked into the material's structure.

This difference matters: moving charge is what later becomes current, but charge itself exists whether or not anything is flowing.

## The smallest useful formal model

The SI unit of charge is the coulomb, written `C`.

The elementary charge has magnitude about:

```text
e = 1.602 x 10^-19 C
```

An electron has charge `-e`. A proton has charge `+e`. A large everyday charge contains an enormous number of elementary charges.

## Think before continuing

If an object gains extra electrons, does its net charge become more positive or more negative? Why?

## Reveal the reasoning

<details>
<summary>Show reasoning</summary>

Electrons carry negative charge. Gaining extra electrons adds more negative charge than positive charge, so the object's net charge becomes negative.

</details>

## Why not the obvious alternative?

It is tempting to describe electricity as a single fluid. That model can sometimes help, but it hides the fact that charge has signs and can cancel. Without signs, attraction, repulsion, and neutral matter become hard to explain.

## Common misconception

Charge is not the same as energy. Charge is a property of matter. Energy describes the ability to cause change. Later, voltage will connect charge and energy by asking how much energy is available per unit of charge.

## Tiny experiment

Rub a plastic comb or balloon on dry hair, then bring it near small paper scraps. The motion is evidence of electrical interaction. The scraps do not need to be part of a complete circuit for charge effects to matter.

## Build or investigation challenge

Make a two-column table: "charge at rest" and "charge moving." List one example or question for each. This prepares the distinction between electric fields and electric current.
